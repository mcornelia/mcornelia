THE 60-SECOND RULES

Fetch. Foil. Find your way home.

  1. Take turns. The starting player alternates each race. Fetch your matching ball, then bring it back to your home square. You can leave and return hours later.
  2. Choose one action. Walk one square up, down, left, or right. Or spend one of your two sprints to travel two clear squares in a straight line. Crossing your ball picks it up; crossing home with it ends your race.
  3. Make a detour. Each of you has six fences. A fence uses your whole turn and blocks one edge for both players, permanently. Tap two adjacent lawn squares to preview it. The game rejects any fence that closes a route to either ball or home.
  4. Share the lawn. Players may pass through or share a square. Flower beds cannot be crossed. Fences cannot be jumped. The garden fences, shown with green rails and square posts, are part of this race’s layout and do not use either player’s fence supply.
  5. Equal chances. If the starting player gets home first, the other player gets one final turn. If both finish in that pair of turns, it’s a tie. Otherwise the first home wins.
  6. A fresh garden. Three tested crossroads gardens are shuffled between races. Both players must pass through the central courtyard at D4. The fences and routes stay fixed during each race. Invite a new race, then wait for your partner to accept. Declining or playing a move cancels the invitation. Neither player can restart alone.

Balls and houses are marked M for Mike and A for Aimee. The step numbers count the remaining walk to your ball and back home, without sprints. Fence previews update those numbers; you find the route.

Tip: A fence that adds one step costs you one turn. Look for a detour that costs your partner more, or save a sprint for the finish.
