Legal & Regulatory Considerations

Not legal advice

This page summarizes publicly available regulatory frameworks that are likely relevant to this collection, gathered during cataloging and research. It is not a legal opinion, not a compliance determination, and not a substitute for review by a qualified wildlife-law attorney or appraiser before any loan, donation, sale, or interstate/international transfer. Material identifications throughout this catalog are visual/presumed, not lab-confirmed, unless explicitly noted otherwise.

Frameworks that likely apply

New York State ivory law
NY ECL §11-0535-a prohibits the sale, offer for sale, purchase, trade, barter, or distribution of elephant ivory and mammoth ivory within New York without a state DEC permit — mammoth ivory is explicitly covered because it can be visually confused with elephant ivory. A narrow antique exception exists (item is 100+ years old, ivory is less than 20% of the object, verified by a qualified appraiser), but permits are required even where an exception applies. This matters for any of this collection's presumed-ivory or mammoth-ivory items if a sale or transfer would occur in or through New York.
Federal African elephant ivory rules (ESA / 4(d) rule)
Commercial import of African elephant ivory is prohibited. Interstate/foreign commercial sale can qualify under two narrow federal exceptions: (1) the ESA antique exception (100+ years old, documented), or (2) the de minimis exception, which requires all three of: ivory component under 200 grams, item manufactured before July 6, 2016, and the ivory removed from the wild before February 26, 1976. A low weight alone (e.g. RHC-055's 140g dealer tag) satisfies only one of the three conditions — the other two still require separate documentation.
Marine Mammal Protection Act / NOAA
Bones, teeth, and ivory from marine mammals (whale, walrus) collected from dead animals cannot simply be commercialized. A Letter of Determination may be required to possess, import, export, or sell protected-species parts, and applicants must document age and origin. Authentic Alaska Native handicrafts made from marine-mammal materials occupy a separate, narrower exempt category — relevant to items like RHC-028 (beluga figure) if Alaska Native origin can be established, but that is not assumed by default.

Recommended next steps

1. Material testing
Non-destructive species/material testing (e.g. UV fluorescence, Schreger-line examination) for the 39 "unresolved" items before any sale, loan, or donation decision.
2. Documentation assembly
Gather any purchase receipts, prior appraisals, family letters, or dealer records that establish acquisition date and origin — this is the single biggest lever for both legal transferability and value.
3. Formal appraisal
A qualified appraiser experienced with wildlife-material antiques can combine art-historical dating with the compliance questions above in one pass.
