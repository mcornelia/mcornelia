Natural-Form Tusk Section with Inset Relief Carving of Scholar and Dragon, on Burl Wood Stand

Object Type
A section of tusk left largely in its natural outer form (bark-like textured exterior, hollow pulp cavity visible at the base end), with a flattened recessed panel carved in relief depicting a robed scholar figure beside a stack of books/scrolls and a maned, scaled dragon; displayed upright on a natural burl wood base/stand.
Material
Elephant ivory tusk section (confirmed by Richard H. Cornelia via prominently visible Schreger lines in the base), natural surface texture retained on the exterior with a visible hollow pulp cavity at the base.
Dimensions (cm)
height: 12.0cm — Overall height ~12.0cm estimated off the cm scale shown in photo b.
Subject Matter
A recessed relief panel showing a standing robed scholar figure beside a tall stack of books or scrolls, accompanied by a maned, scaled dragon coiling below/beside him — a classic Chinese scholar's-object motif pairing literary and mythological imagery, carved into an otherwise naturally-formed tusk section.
Artist Attribution
unknown
Date / Period
undated (stylistic estimate: Chinese carved tusk-section scholar's ornament)
Mount / Display Stand
Displayed upright on a shaped natural burl wood base/stand that fits the piece very closely, indicating it was purpose-made for this specific tusk section rather than a generic or repurposed stand (per T. M. Cornelia) — unlike the repurposed bookstand noted with RHC-053.
Color / Technique
Natural tusk exterior surface largely preserved (bark-like texture) with a single flattened recessed area carved in relief depicting the scholar-and-dragon scene; natural material coloring only, no ink work or paint.
Condition Notes
Exterior shows natural tusk texture and coloring with some age cracking/checking typical of ivory; the base end shows a natural hollow pulp cavity. Not physically examined.
Distinguishing Features
One of the few pieces in the collection retaining the tusk's natural outer form rather than being fully carved or turned into a distinct object shape; the visible hollow pulp cavity at the base is useful physical evidence supporting genuine tusk material; the closely fitted burl wood stand was purpose-made for this piece.
Legal / Regulatory Status
Material not confirmed. If genuine ivory, species and import history would need confirmation before any loan/sale (CITES/ESA considerations). Flag for legal review; not a legal determination.
Rights / Copyright
No attribution identified; copyright status undetermined.

Open Research Questions

Research finding (phase 3, 2026-07-15): Confirmed Chinese carved tusk-section pieces retaining the tusk's natural form (rather than fully worked into a new shape) with inset/relief scholar and dragon imagery are a documented export/decorative category from the Qing dynasty (18th-early 19th century), often made for the Western export market alongside the more famous puzzle balls and card cases; dragon and scholar-scene imagery specifically carried strong auspicious/status symbolism in Chinese decorative art. Supports a Qing-dynasty Chinese export dating estimate consistent with the collection's other Canton-style pieces (RHC-047, RHC-053). Per Richard H. Cornelia (written review of printed catalog booklet, 2026-07-19): "Again, a definite section of elephant ivory. The Schreger lines are prominently shown in the base."
