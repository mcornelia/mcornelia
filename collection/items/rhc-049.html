RHC-049 reviewed

Carved Netsuke: Man Carrying a Ribbed Birdcage on a Pole, Signed with Red Seal Mark(s)

Object Type
A genuine carved netsuke (confirmed by himotoshi cord holes visible on the underside/feet in photo f) depicting a bearded man in richly patterned robes, carrying a ribbed birdcage slung from a curved carrying pole/handle over his shoulder; stylistically and technically consistent with RHC-048 (same two-tone palette, fine incised robe patterning, and red seal marks).
Material
Ivory (confirmed by Richard H. Cornelia via visible texture lines) — two-tone: cream-white for the figure's skin, darker stained tan-brown incised robe, natural lighter tone for the ribbed barrels, with red pigment-filled seal marks.
Dimensions (cm)
length: 6.5cm — Overall length ~6.5cm estimated off the cm scale shown in photo e.
Subject Matter
A bearded man in finely incised patterned robes, bent forward carrying a ribbed birdcage balanced on a curved carrying pole across his shoulders — a genre subject consistent with traditional Japanese netsuke depictions of tradesmen/bird-sellers. The cage's end bears a carved floral (six-petal) roundel design (likely a decorative cage door/roof medallion); the figure's robe bears one or more red seal marks. Feet carved as detailed sandal (waraji) soles, with two differently-sized himotoshi holes visible between them on the underside.
Inscriptions
  • “Illegible red seal mark(s) (artist/workshop signature, characters not confidently read)” (Carved into the robe near the figure's back/shoulder area)
    Same red cinnabar-style signature convention seen on RHC-048; characters not legible at current photo resolution.
Artist Attribution
unknown (signed with unread seal mark(s))
Date / Period
undated (stylistic estimate: Japanese carved netsuke, likely same workshop/tradition as RHC-048)
Mount / Display Stand
None — genuine netsuke, designed to be worn/suspended via cord through the himotoshi holes visible on the underside (photo f) rather than displayed on a stand.
Color / Technique
Two-tone carving with fine incised robe patterning (cross-hatch and floral motifs) and ribbed barrel texture; natural material coloring plus red pigment-filled seal marks; no ink work otherwise.
Condition Notes
Figure appears intact with crisp carved detail across all 6 photos; no visible chips or losses. Not physically examined.
Distinguishing Features
Strong stylistic pairing with RHC-048 (same two-tone technique, robe patterning style, and red seal convention) — likely from the same workshop or a matched netsuke grouping; confirmed genuine netsuke via himotoshi holes visible between the sandaled feet in photo f.
Legal / Regulatory Status
Material not confirmed. If genuine ivory, species and import history would need confirmation before any loan/sale (CITES/ESA considerations, particularly relevant for Japanese ivory netsuke). Flag for legal review; not a legal determination.
Rights / Copyright
Unread artist seal mark(s); copyright/attribution status undetermined.

Open Research Questions

Research finding (phase 3, 2026-07-15): No legible red seal mark was located in the current photo set (unlike RHC-048, where a small seal is visible but unreadable at this resolution) — would need additional macro photography, including the underside/base, to locate and read any mark. Subject confirmed as a man carrying a wicker/ribbed birdcage on a pole — bird-seller and boy-with-birdcage subjects are a documented (if less common than Hotei-type figures) netsuke theme, represented in museum collections (e.g. the Met, Art Gallery of South Australia) from the Edo/Meiji periods, supporting this piece's general dating window even without a workshop attribution. Per Richard H. Cornelia (written review of printed catalog booklet, 2026-07-19): "The two netsuke items [RHC-048 and RHC-049] are ivory as shown by texture lines."
