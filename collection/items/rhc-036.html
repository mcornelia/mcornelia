RHC-036 reviewed

Turned Ivory Needle Case with Needles

Object Type
Sewing implement — a turned ivory/bone cylindrical needle case with a separate screw-on cap; per T. M. Cornelia's identification, the case stores sewing needles inside, and the flat twin-pronged, double-eyed metal forms visible in RHC-036-b are a pair of needles (their eyes visible) partially withdrawn from the case rather than a fixed tool tip.
Material
Ivory (per Richard H. Cornelia) for the case and cap; steel sewing needles inside, showing some reddish surface staining/oxidation.
Dimensions (cm)
length: 8.0cm — Assembled length (cap + handle) ~8.0cm, estimated off the cm scale shown in RHC-036-a; handle and cap are similar length, joined by a raised turned ring at the midpoint.
Subject Matter
Not applicable — a plain turned utilitarian case and cap, tapered/bullet-shaped at the outer ends with a raised ring at the joint, no figural or narrative decoration.
Artist Attribution
not applicable
Date / Period
19th century (per Richard H. Cornelia — dated by the style of the included needles).
Mount / Display Stand
None — unmounted, two-piece case (body + separate screw-on cap), photographed both assembled (RHC-036-a) and open (RHC-036-b) with the cap removed to show needles withdrawn from the case.
Color / Technique
Plain turned form with a single raised ring at the case/cap joint; natural material coloring only, no ink work or paint.
Condition Notes
Case and cap appear intact and glossy; the needles show light reddish staining/oxidation. Not physically examined.
Distinguishing Features
Second protective-capped implement in the collection (after RHC-035), sharing the same bullet-tapered body/cap silhouette but functioning as a needle case rather than a pen — the two pieces may be part of a matched ivory-turned sewing/writing implement set. Richard H. Cornelia notes the threaded ivory connection joining the cap and bottom would have been technically difficult to carve — a notable maker's skill detail.
Legal / Regulatory Status
Material not confirmed. If genuine ivory, age/species would need confirmation before any loan/sale. Flag for legal review; not a legal determination.
Rights / Copyright
Utilitarian object; no attribution or copyright concern anticipated.

Open Research Questions

Research finding (phase 3, 2026-07-15): Confirmed turned ivory needle cases (ring-turned or decoratively carved) are a well-documented Victorian sewing-accessory category, often sold individually rather than only as sets — doesn't resolve whether this piece and RHC-035 were an original matched set, but confirms the object type/period placement independently. Per Richard H. Cornelia (written review of printed catalog booklet, 2026-07-19): "The included needles indicate 19th century. Carving the threaded ivory connection for the cap and bottom must have been difficult."
