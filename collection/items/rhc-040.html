Polychrome Engraved Cigarette Holder with Chinese Dragon and Calligraphy

Object Type
Cigarette holder — a cylindrical tube with a hollow bore at one end (showing dark tar/tobacco staining) and a small metal fitting visible at the mouthpiece end, engraved along its body with a polychrome dragon scene and Chinese characters.
Material
Ivory (per Richard H. Cornelia's collection-wide statement that there are no bone items in the collection), cream-white cylindrical body; small metal (brass or steel) fitting at one end.
Dimensions (cm)
length: 12.5cm — Overall length (~12.5cm) estimated off the cm scale shown in RHC-040-a.
Subject Matter
A Chinese-style dragon, rendered in green polychrome with red/orange facial accents, coiling along the length of the holder, accompanied by a vertical column of Chinese characters (calligraphic inscription, not confidently legible/translated from the photos).
Inscriptions
  • “Vertical column of Chinese characters” (engraved along the body near the dragon scene)
    Not confidently legible or translated from available photos; likely a poem, maker's inscription, or auspicious phrase typical of Chinese export scrimshaw/carved goods.
Artist Attribution
unknown
Date / Period
undated (stylistic estimate: Chinese export piece, 19th-20th c.)
Mount / Display Stand
None — unmounted, freestanding cigarette holder.
Color / Technique
Polychrome engraved (scrimshaw-style) dragon scene in green and red/orange ink, with fine-line Chinese calligraphy; hollow bore at the insertion end shows dark staining consistent with tobacco use.
Condition Notes
Body appears intact with clear, well-preserved polychrome coloring; the bore shows expected use staining. Not physically examined.
Distinguishing Features
Second cigarette holder in the collection (after RHC-039), and the first Chinese-style engraved/polychrome scrimshaw piece with legible (if untranslated) Chinese script — connects to RHC-037's East Asian export letter opener as further evidence of an Asian-sourced sub-group within the collection.

Open Research Questions

Research finding (phase 3, 2026-07-15): Direct photo review confirms columns of Chinese calligraphy alongside the dragon motif, but the characters are too small/low-resolution in the current photo set to transcribe or translate reliably (would risk a false reading) — needs a macro close-up photo of the inscription specifically before attempting translation. Ties stylistically to RHC-037 (East Asian export carving) as part of the same Asian-export sub-group, supporting a shared Chinese export source for both. Per Richard H. Cornelia (written review of printed catalog booklet, 2026-07-19): Richard H. Cornelia's written review states as a collection-wide fact: "There are no bone items; bone is clearly characterized by dark, black flecks of dried blood in the pores of the item. I always steered clear of bone; concentrating on ivory instead." Applied here to resolve this item's previous ivory-or-bone hedge in favor of ivory. Additionally, on long undecorated items like this he noted: "They are all ivory, but I'd need to re-examine them to determine species, elephant, mammoth or walrus which I think is not too important. Identifying them as ivory is sufficient."
