Polychrome Engraved Tooth, Raven with Pacific Northwest Coast-Style Totem Pole, on Wood Base

Object Type
Engraved tooth, mounted on a wood display base via a short peg
Material
Presumed tooth (conical curved form, cream-white glossy surface). Given the Pacific Northwest Coast Native/Alaska Native-influenced subject matter (see below), walrus ivory is also plausible alongside sperm whale tooth — both are traditional media for this art style — so material is flagged as uncertain between the two rather than assumed to match the rest of the collection.
Dimensions (cm)

Subject Matter
A raven perched in the foreground, rendered in solid black with fine feather detailing, set against a background element styled after a Pacific Northwest Coast Native (e.g. Tlingit/Haida/Northwest Coast) totem pole or formline carving — large stylized eye/face shapes with red and yellow/gold accent coloring, clearly distinct from the ship/whale/wildlife scenes elsewhere in the collection. Polychrome technique (black ink line work with red, yellow/gold, and brown color washes).

Worth handling thoughtfully given the cultural specificity of the subject matter (raven and formline art carry significant meaning in Northwest Coast Indigenous traditions) when researched or described further.

Open Research Questions

Research finding (phase 3, 2026-07-15): Confirmed Alaska Native (walrus/mammoth ivory) scrimshaw and Pacific Northwest Coast totem carving are two distinct real traditions that plausibly explain this piece's raven-with-totem-pole imagery — Alaska Native coastal peoples have carved and scrimshawed walrus ivory for millennia (protected/regulated under the 1972 Marine Mammal Protection Act, legally harvestable only by Alaska Natives), while totem-pole imagery specifically belongs to Northwest Coast tribes (Tlingit, Haida, Tsimshian, etc.) further south — meaning this piece likely represents a commercial/tourist-market blending of the two visual traditions (a common phenomenon in 20th-century Alaska/NW Coast souvenir carving) rather than a single unified cultural origin. No specific workshop/maker identified.
