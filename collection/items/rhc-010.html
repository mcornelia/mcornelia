RHC-010 reviewed

Reclining Mermaid Engraved Fossil Walrus Tooth on Turned Pedestal Base

Object Type
Engraved tooth, mounted on a turned wood pedestal/stem base (distinct from the flat-puck mounts seen on RHC-005 through RHC-008)
Material
Fossil walrus tooth (confirmed by Richard H. Cornelia — not a sperm whale tooth as previously presumed), with one side polished to reveal clear ivory. Base is a dark-stained wood pedestal with a turned baluster-style stem and a round foot.
Dimensions (cm)
length: 6.0cm — Tooth length (~6.0cm) measured off the cm scale; width and base/pedestal dimensions not separately measured — ruler only runs along one axis in these photos.
Subject Matter
A reclining mermaid on a beach/rocky shore, with small fish and a shell or starfish above her, rendered in black ink line work with cross-hatched shading.
Artist Attribution
unknown (per Richard H. Cornelia)
Date / Period
Undated; no signature found on close inspection.
Mount / Display Stand
Mounted for display on a turned wood pedestal base with a baluster-style stem — the most elaborate mount style seen so far, distinct from the flat disk/puck mounts on RHC-005 through RHC-008.
Color / Technique
Traditional black ink line scrimshaw engraving with cross-hatched shading
Condition Notes
Root end has a rough, natural tan-brown textured surface (not damage); engraved face shows light natural mottling. No visible cracks or losses. Not physically examined.
Distinguishing Features
Mermaid/maritime fantasy subject matter, distinct from the wildlife and lighthouse themes elsewhere in the collection. Pedestal-style mount is the most ornate seen so far.
Legal / Regulatory Status
Tooth material presumed sperm whale (same flag as RHC-001) — undated, so pre/post-1972 MMPA status can't be determined from the piece itself. Flag for legal review before any loan/sale; not a legal determination.
Rights / Copyright
No signature identified; copyright status undetermined.
Keywords

reviewed

Open Research Questions

Research finding (phase 3, 2026-07-15): See RHC-005 — turned pedestal/rod-and-base mounts across RHC-005 through RHC-011 are plausibly from a shared commercial supplier rather than individually custom-made; no specific source identified. Per Richard H. Cornelia (written review of printed catalog booklet, 2026-07-19): "A fossil walrus tooth. One side has been polished to gain clear ivory. Artist unknown."
