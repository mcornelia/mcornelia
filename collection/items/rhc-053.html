RHC-053 reviewed

Chinese "Canton-Style" Carved Ivory Visiting/Calling Card Case (Damaged, Two Fitting/Joining Panels), Displayed in Repurposed Wood Bookstand

Object Type
A hollow, densely carved and pierced ivory box in the Chinese export "Canton" tradition, sized to hold Victorian-era visiting/calling cards (too large for standard playing cards) — essentially a flattened box formed from two fitting/joining carved panels (front and back walls). The case is damaged: the top edges on both short sides are mostly missing, consistent with loss of the case's lid/closure structure. The red wood stand seen in the photos is a separate, repurposed bookstand used only for display, not an integral mount.
Material
Presumed ivory (cream-white, densely carved with fine pierced openwork throughout, light natural tan staining in recesses).
Dimensions (cm)
length: 11.0cm — Case body ~11.0cm wide overall (front/back panels); estimated off the cm scale shown in photo c — case would originally have had additional height/depth from its now-missing top/closure structure.
Subject Matter
A crowded, multi-register Chinese village or courtly genre scene carved in extremely dense relief: pavilions and buildings with tiled roofs, numerous small figures engaged in daily activities, trees, and dense pierced floral/foliate borders — a hallmark composition of 19th-century Chinese export 'Canton' carved ivory work.
Artist Attribution
unknown
Date / Period
undated (stylistic estimate: 19th-century Chinese export "Canton" carved ivory)
Mount / Display Stand
Displayed upright in a red wood stand for photography, but per Mike Cornelia this is a repurposed bookstand, not part of the object or an integral mount.
Color / Technique
Extremely dense, multi-layered pierced relief carving in the Canton style, with figures, architecture, and foliage carved at different depths for a layered effect; natural material coloring only, no ink work or paint.
Condition Notes
Damaged per Mike Cornelia: the top edges of the case on both short sides are mostly missing, consistent with loss of the original lid/closure structure; a visible age crack/fissure also runs partway up the larger panel's face. The two carved panels are fitting/joining pieces of the case body (front and back walls) rather than separate unrelated objects. Not physically examined.
Distinguishing Features
Densest and most figurally complex carved ivory work in the collection to date, representing the classic Chinese export 'Canton' carving tradition applied to a functional visiting/calling card case rather than a purely decorative panel; damaged with the case's top/closure structure largely missing.
Legal / Regulatory Status
Material not confirmed. If genuine ivory, species and import history would need confirmation before any loan/sale (CITES/ESA considerations for Chinese export ivory). Flag for legal review; not a legal determination.
