Carved Figural Letter Opener / Page Turner, Robed Figure Handle (East Asian Style)

Object Type
Letter opener / paper knife (also known as a page turner — the same form of blunt-bladed knife was historically used both to open letters and to slit uncut book pages) with a long tapered blade and a carved figural handle depicting a standing robed figure.
Material
Presumed ivory (smooth cream-white surface, fine carving detail); species not confirmed.
Dimensions (cm)
length: 22.5cm — Overall length (~22.5cm) estimated off the cm scale shown in the photos — the longest single-piece carved object in the collection so far.
Subject Matter
A standing figure in a long flowing robe, smiling, holding an object (possibly a basket, fan, or bundle) at waist height in one hand — carving style and costume suggest an East Asian (Chinese or Japanese export) figural tradition, consistent with late 19th-early 20th c. carved ivory letter openers made for the Western export/tourist market.
Artist Attribution
unknown
Date / Period
undated (stylistic estimate: 19th-early 20th c. East Asian export carving)
Mount / Display Stand
None — unmounted, freestanding letter opener.
Color / Technique
Fully carved figural handle with fine robe-fold and facial detail, transitioning to a plain flat tapered blade; natural material coloring only, no ink work or paint.
Condition Notes
Blade and figural handle appear intact with no visible chips or cracks; surface has a smooth aged patina. Not physically examined.
Distinguishing Features
First letter opener/paper knife in the collection, and the first piece suggesting a specifically East Asian (rather than American whaling or Alaska Native) carving tradition — a notable outlier in origin compared to most of the collection.
Legal / Regulatory Status
Material not confirmed. If genuine ivory, age/species and import history would need confirmation before any loan/sale (CITES/ESA considerations for non-domestic ivory). Flag for legal review; not a legal determination.
Rights / Copyright
No attribution identified; copyright status undetermined.

Open Research Questions

Research finding (phase 3, 2026-07-15): Fits the broader Chinese Canton export ivory tradition (see RHC-053/RHC-047 research notes) — Canton workshops produced a wide range of small carved-ivory export novelties for Western buyers beyond card cases and puzzle balls, including figural-handle letter openers/paper knives, typically 19th century. No specific maker or dating mark found; general style and export-market context support a 19th-century Chinese export origin rather than a purely decorative American/European piece.
