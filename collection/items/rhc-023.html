Naval Battle Scene, Two Frigates Exchanging Cannon Fire, Engraved Hippopotamus Canine on Two-Post Wood Base

Object Type
Engraved tooth, mounted horizontally on a round wood display base via two short posts
Material
Hippopotamus lower canine (confirmed by Richard H. Cornelia — not a sperm whale tooth as previously presumed; identified via the vertical concave groove running the piece's length, a diagnostic hippo-canine feature). Hippo ivory is rare in scrimshaw due to its exceptionally hard enamel, which is difficult to engrave.
Dimensions (cm)
length: 13.5cm, width: 4.0cm — Length (~13.5cm) and width/diameter (~4.0cm) estimated off the two cm scales shown in the photos.
Subject Matter
A naval battle scene: two full-rigged square-sailed frigates facing each other, exchanging cannon fire, with smoke/spray bursts rendered as fine stippled particle clouds between them. A ship's flag (appears to be a U.S. flag) flies from the vessel at right. Both ships are rendered in detailed black ink line work with cross-hatched shading, no color, set within a bordered oval-band composition typical of classic scrimshaw tooth engravings.

No signature found despite the fine level of detail.

Flag explicitly for legal review before any loan/sale, since the applicable regulatory regime differs from most of the rest of the collection.

Open Research Questions

Research finding (phase 3, 2026-07-15): General web search did not surface a specific historical naval engagement or ship identification matching this scene without more detail (flag colors/national ensigns visible in the engraving would be needed to narrow it down further, per the original research note) — this one likely needs direct visual comparison against period naval-engagement prints/paintings rather than a text search. Per Richard H. Cornelia (written review of printed catalog booklet, 2026-07-19): "The vertical concave groove running the length of the piece indicates this to be a hippopotamus lower canine." General ID reference (from Richard's booklet preamble): elephant and mammoth ivory both show cross-hatched Schreger lines (~90 degrees for mammoth, ~115 degrees for elephant), most visible on a polished surface; elephant ivory also has concentric growth-ring circles and a hollow nerve-cavity core along roughly the first third of a tusk's length; mammoth ivory, being fossil/petrified, is typically stained brownish from long burial and is almost never white/cream; walrus tusk has a prominent mottled/jumbled darker-dentine core; sperm whale teeth are cream-colored with a triangular hollow core; hippopotamus canines show strong curvature, an oval cross-section, and prominent concave grooves along their length, with enamel too hard to easily fake or etch.
