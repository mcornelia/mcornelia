RHC-033 reviewed

Fine-Tooth Mustache or Beard Comb with Separate Riveted Handle

Object Type
Small fine-tooth double-sided comb (teeth cut on both top and bottom edges) fitted with a separate slender curved handle, joined to the comb body by what appear to be two small metal rivets/pins and a reddish adhesive or shellac at the joint. Per T. M. Cornelia, the small size and handled form are more consistent with a personal grooming comb (mustache or beard comb) than a nit/lice comb.
Material
Ivory (per Richard H. Cornelia's collection-wide statement that there are no bone items in the collection) for both the comb head and handle, cream-white coloring; the handle appears to be a separate piece from the comb body.
Dimensions (cm)
length: 9.5cm — Overall length (~9.5cm, handle tip to comb edge) estimated off the cm scale shown in the photo; comb head is roughly 4.5cm wide.
Subject Matter
A small, personal-grooming fine-tooth comb, rectangular head with narrow, closely-spaced teeth cut on both the top and bottom edges, fitted with a slender, gently curved handle extending from the center of one long edge — likely used for grooming a mustache or beard rather than as a hair/lice comb, given its small scale and dedicated handle.
Artist Attribution
not applicable
Date / Period
undated (stylistic estimate: form consistent with 19th-early 20th c. gentlemen's grooming combs)
Mount / Display Stand
None — unmounted, freestanding object.
Color / Technique
Plain, undecorated utilitarian object; no engraving, ink work, or carving beyond the functional comb teeth. A reddish-brown residue is visible at the handle/comb joint, likely old adhesive or shellac rather than decorative color.
Condition Notes
Comb teeth appear intact and evenly spaced on both edges; the handle-to-comb joint shows visible rivets/pins and adhesive residue, suggesting either an original composite construction or an old repair. Not physically examined.
Distinguishing Features
The first comb in the collection, and a purely functional/utilitarian personal grooming object rather than a decorative or engraved scrimshaw piece — similar in category to the snow knife (RHC-024) and corset busk (RHC-025) as non-decorative items. The riveted, separately-made handle is a notable construction detail worth further examination.
Legal / Regulatory Status
Material not confirmed. If genuine ivory, age/species would need confirmation before any loan/sale. Flag for legal review; not a legal determination.
Rights / Copyright
Utilitarian object; no attribution or copyright concern anticipated.

Open Research Questions

Research finding (phase 3, 2026-07-15): Confirmed mustache/beard combs were a genuine, status-conscious Victorian/Edwardian men's grooming accessory — ivory versions in particular 'represented serious money' rather than everyday utility items, with fine detail carving meant to be shown off (contemporary period pricing for a comparable silver/tortoiseshell example ran to roughly $70 in today's money). Supports this piece being a genuine period grooming article rather than a novelty; the riveted-handle construction question (original vs. repair) still needs physical inspection. Per Richard H. Cornelia (written review of printed catalog booklet, 2026-07-19): Richard H. Cornelia's written review states as a collection-wide fact: "There are no bone items; bone is clearly characterized by dark, black flecks of dried blood in the pores of the item. I always steered clear of bone; concentrating on ivory instead." Applied here to resolve this item's previous ivory-or-bone hedge in favor of ivory. Additionally, on long undecorated items like this he noted: "They are all ivory, but I'd need to re-examine them to determine species, elephant, mammoth or walrus which I think is not too important. Identifying them as ivory is sufficient."
