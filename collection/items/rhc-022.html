RHC-022 reviewed

Polychrome Engraved Grizzly Bear Scene, Monogrammed 'B[?] '81, Edition 1-14, on Cross-Bar Wood Pedestal

Object Type
Engraved rectangular block/bar (tusk section, not a whole tooth), mounted on a wood pedestal base with a cross-bar support (distinct from the peg/rod mounts used elsewhere)
Material
Presumed walrus ivory, cut as a rectangular block/bar section — not a whole tooth. The form is flat-sided with rounded corners rather than curved/conical (as sperm whale teeth are), and the end face (RHC-022-d) shows a rough, chalky, mottled cross-section texture rather than a smooth glossy root, both more consistent with a sawn tusk section than a whole tooth. Species/material not confirmed by any label; this is a visual inference only.
Dimensions (cm)
length: 15.5cm, width: 6.0cm — Length (~15.5cm) and width (~6.0cm) estimated off the two cm scales shown in the photos; notably larger than most other teeth in the collection.
Subject Matter
A roaring grizzly bear standing over a driftwood log and rocks beside a stream, rendered in fine black ink line work with naturalistic brown color washes on the bear's fur (polychrome) and uncolored line work in the rocky/wooded foreground. A bordered rectangular framing line surrounds the main scene, similar in presentation style to a formal 'plate' composition.
Inscriptions
  • “Stylized interlocking monogram cipher (letters not confidently resolved, possibly 'B' combined with 'A' or 'V'), dated '81” (engraved at the lower right of the scene, just inside the border)
    A carefully rendered monogram-style artist's cipher rather than a plain signature; year '81 (1981) is clearly legible.
  • “1-14” (engraved directly below the monogram)
    Likely an edition/limited-run number (e.g. '1 of 14'), though could alternatively be a date notation; not confidently resolved.
  • “Small decorative scroll/flourish mark” (engraved below the '1-14' inscription)
    A separate stylized swash or flourish, possibly a secondary maker's mark or decorative sign-off distinct from the monogram above.
Artist Attribution
unknown (monogrammed 'B[?]', dated 1981, cipher not confidently resolved)
Date / Period
1981 (dated in monogram)
Mount / Display Stand
Mounted on a round wood pedestal base via a cross-bar/bridge support rather than a simple peg — a distinct mount style not seen elsewhere in the collection so far.
Color / Technique
Black ink line scrimshaw engraving with naturalistic brown polychrome color washes on the bear, uncolored line work elsewhere
Condition Notes
Some natural mottling/discoloration is visible on the root end of the tooth (upper left in the primary photo); engraved face otherwise appears clean and intact. Not physically examined.
Distinguishing Features
Notably larger than most teeth in the collection (~15.5cm) and mounted on a unique cross-bar pedestal stand. The elaborate interlocking monogram cipher, firm 1981 date, and possible edition number ('1-14') suggest a deliberately produced, possibly numbered/limited work rather than an informal or one-off piece — worth comparing to other monogrammed or dated pieces if any turn up later in the collection.
Legal / Regulatory Status
If confirmed walrus ivory (modern, not fossil, given the fresh/glossy engraved surface), subject to MMPA restrictions on walrus ivory, with an exemption for Alaska Native-produced authentic native handicrafts; the 1981 engraving date does not itself establish when the raw material was obtained. Material not confirmed. Flag for legal review before any loan/sale; not a legal determination.
Rights / Copyright
Monogrammed and dated 1981; artist not yet identified by name — copyright status undetermined pending identification.

Open Research Questions

Research finding (phase 3, 2026-07-15): No public record found matching an interlocking 'B'/'A'/'V' monogram cipher to a known scrimshaw artist active circa 1981. This kind of personal-monogram (vs. printed studio label) signature is typical of independent/hobbyist scrimshanders from that era who are not necessarily documented online; a physical-archive or regional-collector-club lookup would be needed to go further than a general web search allows.
