Small Carved Ivory Object with Dense Openwork Floral Carving (Netsuke Uncertain)

Object Type
A small, compact, densely carved object with openwork/pierced floral or foliate carving and a beaded rope-like border. Small size and compact form would be consistent with a netsuke, but per T. M. Cornelia, the piece lacks the two differently-sized himotoshi (cord) holes expected on a genuine netsuke, so this identification remains uncertain; the dark openings visible in photos appear to be undercuts/voids in the openwork carving rather than deliberate himotoshi.
Material
Presumed ivory (cream-white, dense fine carving with some darker tan staining in the recessed/pierced areas).
Dimensions (cm)
length: 3.0cm — Overall size (~3.0cm) estimated off the cm scale shown in the photos — one of the smallest carved objects in the collection.
Subject Matter
Not confidently identified — dense openwork/pierced carving suggesting foliage, flowers, or a stylized animal/figural form, with a beaded rope-like border running along part of the piece. Subject and orientation not clear from the two available photo angles.

Condition Notes
Appears intact; the piercings/openings appear to be decorative openwork rather than himotoshi or damage, though this is not certain from photos. Not physically examined.

Cornelia) leaves this identification unresolved — subject matter and function still need confirmation.

Open Research Questions

Research finding (phase 3, 2026-07-15): Direct photo review of both available photos found no himotoshi (cord-attachment holes) anywhere on the piece — this supports classifying it as an okimono (purely decorative carving) or a fragment of a larger carved object rather than a genuine netsuke, consistent with the original cataloging note. The dark rectangular inset areas visible in both photos look like a separate insert (possibly a different material or a broken/missing carved element) rather than part of the intended floral design — worth a physical check to see if something is missing or embedded there.
