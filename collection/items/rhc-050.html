RHC-050 reviewed

Carved Ivory Chess Piece: Mounted Warrior/Huntsman on Rearing Horse, with Scroll-Carved Rectangular Base

Object Type
A carved ivory chess piece (per Richard H. Cornelia — one piece from a chess set, not a standalone sculptural group) depicting a mounted figure on a rearing horse raising a weapon overhead, together with a separate rectangular carved plinth/box element with scrolling foliate relief.
Material
Presumed ivory (cream-white, finely detailed carving with light natural mottling).
Dimensions (cm)
length: 9.0cm — Rider/horse group ~9.0cm long; rectangular plinth element roughly 3.5cm wide; estimated off the cm scale shown in photos a and b.
Subject Matter
A robed, bearded rider mounted on a rearing horse, one arm raised overhead brandishing a curved weapon (sword or club), the horse shown mid-rear with front legs raised and head turned — a dynamic hunting or battle scene composition. The horse wears a detailed carved harness/saddle blanket with cross-hatch and scale patterning. Alongside is a separate rectangular plinth or box element carved with a scrolling acanthus/foliate relief band along its visible side.
Artist Attribution
unknown
Date / Period
undated (stylistic estimate: South/Southeast Asian carved ivory, possibly Indian or Persian-influenced equestrian hunting scene)
Mount / Display Stand
Uncertain — photographed adjacent to a separate rectangular carved plinth/box element; not clear whether the rider group originally sat atop this plinth (similar in concept to RHC-043's separate base) or whether the two are unrelated pieces photographed together.
Color / Technique
Fully carved sculptural form with fine detailed texture (horse mane/tail, rider's robe folds, harness patterning) on the figural group, and separate scrolling relief carving on the rectangular plinth; natural material coloring only, no ink work or paint.
Condition Notes
Figural group appears intact with fine, delicate elements (raised weapon arm, horse legs) preserved with no visible breaks; the rectangular plinth also appears intact. Not physically examined.
Distinguishing Features
Most dynamic action-pose sculptural figure group in the collection to date (rearing horse, raised weapon), continuing the fully three-dimensional sculpture sub-group (RHC-027 through RHC-031, RHC-043); the accompanying rectangular scroll-carved plinth is a new element type not seen with the earlier animal sculptures, raising the question of whether it originally served as this piece's base.
Legal / Regulatory Status
Material not confirmed. If genuine ivory, species and import history would need confirmation before any loan/sale (CITES/ESA considerations). Flag for legal review; not a legal determination.
Acquisition Info
Purchased by Richard H. Cornelia at an antique shop on Route 7 in western Massachusetts. Other pieces from the same chess set were not available for purchase, so this and RHC-052 are orphaned pieces rather than a complete set.
Rights / Copyright
No attribution identified; copyright status undetermined.

Open Research Questions

Research finding (phase 3, 2026-07-15): General web search did not find a specific match for this exact mounted-warrior form, but confirmed carved ivory equestrian/rearing-horse warrior figures are a documented category spanning multiple traditions — Qing dynasty Chinese carved ivory 'warrior on horse' figures are an established auction/collector category, and equestrian hunting imagery (horse-mounted riders) is also a long Persian/Mughal decorative-art theme. Given the rest of the collection's strong Chinese-export presence (RHC-047, 053, 054, 042), a Qing-dynasty Chinese origin is the more likely fit for this pair than Persian, though this remains a style-comparison judgment rather than a confirmed attribution. Per Richard H. Cornelia (written review of printed catalog booklet, 2026-07-19): "Both pieces [RHC-050 and RHC-052] are ivory carvings from a chess board. Purchased in western Massachusetts (route 7 antique shop); other pieces were not available." This reidentifies these as orphaned chess pieces from an incomplete set, not standalone sculptural figures.
