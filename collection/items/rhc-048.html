RHC-048 reviewed

Carved Netsuke: Seated Man with Sack, Signed with Red Seal Mark

Object Type
A genuine carved netsuke (confirmed by the presence of two differently-sized himotoshi cord holes bored through the sack/bundle element) depicting a seated bearded man in robes leaning against a large ribbed sack or bundle, the sack carved in a darker stained tone contrasting with the lighter figure, bearing a red cinnabar seal/signature mark.
Material
Presumed ivory (two-tone: cream-white for the figure's skin/robes, darker stained tan-brown for the sack/bundle), with a red pigment-filled seal mark.
Dimensions (cm)
length: 6.5cm — Overall length ~6.5cm estimated off the cm scale shown in photo a.
Subject Matter
A seated, bearded elderly man in flowing robes, one hand raised near his chin/beard, leaning back against a large ribbed cloth sack or bundle secured with a wrapped cord/strap — a composition consistent with traditional Japanese netsuke subjects such as a traveling merchant, sage, or Hotei-like figure with his bag of treasures. A red rectangular seal mark is carved/inset into the sack, functioning as a maker's signature.
Inscriptions
  • “Illegible red seal mark (artist/workshop signature, characters not confidently read)” (Carved rectangular cartouche on the sack, adjacent to the himotoshi holes)
    Classic red cinnabar-style netsuke signature seal; characters not legible at current photo resolution.
Artist Attribution
unknown (signed with an unread seal mark)
Date / Period
undated (stylistic estimate: Japanese carved netsuke)
Mount / Display Stand
None — genuine netsuke, designed to be worn/suspended via cord through the himotoshi holes rather than displayed on a stand.
Color / Technique
Two-tone carving contrasting light figure against a darker stained sack, with fine surface detail (robe folds, sack ribbing, facial features); a red pigment-filled seal mark; two himotoshi holes of clearly different diameters bored through the sack.
Condition Notes
Figure appears intact with crisp carved detail; the himotoshi holes and seal show no visible damage. Not physically examined.

Open Research Questions

Research finding (phase 3, 2026-07-15): Iconography confirmed via direct photo review — this is Hotei (布袋), one of the Seven Lucky Gods of Japanese folk Buddhism, depicted in his classic pose seated against his signature cloth sack (his name literally means 'cloth bag'); as an itinerant monk figure he distributes the sack's contents to the needy, and he symbolizes abundance, contentment, and good fortune, making him one of the most common netsuke subjects. A small red seal mark is visible on the back of the sack in photo RHC-048-a but is too small/low-resolution in the current photo set to transcribe reliably — would need a macro close-up shot to attempt a translation/workshop ID.
