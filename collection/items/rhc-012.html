RHC-012 reviewed

Matched Pair of Polychrome Scrimshaw Teeth, Coastal/Nautical Scenes with Marine Life, on Shared Double-Peg Wood Base

Object Type
Two engraved teeth mounted together on a single shared turned wood display base via two dark pegs (a matched pair, not two separate mounts)
Material
Sperm whale teeth (confirmed by Richard H. Cornelia) — matched pair. The exposed dentine at the tips, where enamel has worn away, is typical of genuine sperm whale teeth. Base is a dark-stained turned wood puck, drilled with two peg holes to hold both teeth side by side.
Dimensions (cm)
length: 12.5cm — Length (~12.5cm) estimated off the cm scale for the upper tooth; the lower tooth appears similar in length. Both teeth are shown at an angle relative to the ruler, so this is an approximation, not a precise measurement; width/base dimensions not separately measured.
Subject Matter
A matched pair, each tooth divided into two linked vignettes within a single bordered panel: the upper tooth shows a densely-rigged sailing ship/harbor scene in muted ink at left, transitioning into a pair of dolphins or small whales swimming amid coral/anemone accents in pink and orange at right. The lower tooth shows a lighthouse and rolling green hillside scene with a red/orange sun at left, transitioning into a single leaping dolphin or orca amid similar coral accents at right. Rendered in polychrome (black ink line work with hand-colored green, red/orange, and pink washes), similar coloring technique to the thimble RHC-004 but on a much larger, more elaborate scale.
Artist Attribution
unknown
Date / Period
Undated; no signature found in the blank ivory margins checked near the root of either tooth.
Mount / Display Stand
Both teeth mounted together on a single shared, dark-stained turned wood base via two dark pegs inserted into drilled holes at each tooth's root — a double-tooth mount style not seen elsewhere in the collection so far.
Color / Technique
Polychrome scrimshaw: black ink line engraving with hand-applied color washes (green, red/orange, pink) — same general technique family as RHC-004 but a much larger and more complex composition on each tooth.
Condition Notes
Both teeth show damaged/broken root ends — rough, jagged, unpolished breaks rather than smooth natural roots or clean cuts, clearly visible where each tooth meets its mounting peg (see close-up). This looks like old damage rather than a manufacturing feature, and is worth flagging for condition assessment. A natural vertical crack with brown staining is visible in the blank ivory margin of the upper tooth, below the coral border. Engraved/colored surfaces otherwise appear intact. Not physically examined.
Distinguishing Features
The only matched pair in the collection so far (two teeth sharing one mount), and the most elaborate polychrome compositions seen to date — each tooth combines a distinct coastal/nautical vignette with a marine-life vignette in one continuous bordered scene. Root-end damage on both teeth is notable and distinct from the clean or naturally rough (but undamaged) roots seen on single-tooth items.
Legal / Regulatory Status
Tooth material presumed sperm whale (same flag as RHC-001) — undated, so pre/post-1972 MMPA status can't be determined from the pieces themselves. Flag for legal review before any loan/sale; not a legal determination.
Rights / Copyright
No signature identified; copyright status undetermined.

Open Research Questions

Research finding (phase 3, 2026-07-15): Matched pairs on a shared double-peg base, like this one, were a common presentation format for commercially sold scrimshaw sets — supports the 'commissioned or sold as a set' theory in the original note, though no specific source/maker was identified via general search. Per Richard H. Cornelia (written review of printed catalog booklet, 2026-07-19): "A matched pair of sperm whale teeth. The exposed dentine at the tips where the enamel has been worn away is typical of sperm whale teeth."
