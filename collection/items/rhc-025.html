Page Turner with Ornate Blue-Ink Monogram Flourish, Initials "S.O.E." (tentative reading)

Object Type
Page turner — a flat, straight bookbinding/reading tool used to slit or turn pages, per T. M. Cornelia's correction (2026-07-15); previously misidentified as a corset busk, but busks are curved to fit the body and this piece is straight, ruling that out.
Material
Ivory (per Richard H. Cornelia's collection-wide statement that there are no bone items in the collection — he steered clear of bone and concentrated on ivory throughout), cream-white with a fine natural grain pattern visible along its length. Species (elephant/mammoth/walrus) not determined; per Richard, identifying long items like this as ivory is sufficient without further species breakdown.
Dimensions (cm)
length: 25.0cm, width: 2.5cm — Length (~25.0cm) and width (~2.5cm) estimated off the cm scales shown in the photos — proportions consistent with typical 19th-century corset busks.
Subject Matter
No pictorial scene — the piece is otherwise entirely plain/undecorated apart from a single ornate calligraphic flourish in blue ink near one end.
Inscriptions
  • “S.O.E. (tentative reading, per T. M. Cornelia)” (in blue ink near one end of the strip's face)
    An interlocking monogram of initials rendered in heavy Spencerian/copperplate-style calligraphic flourish, likely belonging to the intended recipient (sweetheart) of the busk. Read by T. M. Cornelia as 'S.O.E.'; the extreme decorative looping made this difficult to resolve independently.
Artist Attribution
unknown

undated (stylistic estimate: 19th century, consistent with sailor-made or Victorian-era personal ivory/bone keepsakes bearing monogram flourishes)

Condition Notes
Surface appears clean, glossy, and intact with no visible cracks or damage. Not physically examined.
Distinguishing Features
Unlike nearly every other piece in the collection, this object bears no pictorial scene at all — only a monogrammed initials flourish. Straight profile confirms it as a page turner rather than a corset busk (busks are curved); the monogram is still consistent with a personal sweetheart/keepsake gift, just in a different object category than originally catalogued.
Legal / Regulatory Status
Material and species not confirmed (ivory vs. bone). If ivory postdates 1972, MMPA restrictions would apply; if antique/pre-1972 (consistent with a 19th-century busk), different rules apply. Flag for legal review before any loan/sale; not a legal determination.
