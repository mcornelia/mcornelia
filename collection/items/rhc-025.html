Corset Busk with Ornate Blue-Ink Monogram Flourish, Initials "S.O.E." (tentative reading)

Object Type
Corset busk — a flat stiffening strip inserted into a 19th-century woman's corset, confirmed identification (per Mike Cornelia); a well-documented type of sailor-made scrimshaw sweetheart gift.
Material
Ivory or bone (presumed), cream-white with a fine natural grain pattern visible along its length
Dimensions (cm)
length: 25.0cm, width: 2.5cm — Length (~25.0cm) and width (~2.5cm) estimated off the cm scales shown in the photos — proportions consistent with typical 19th-century corset busks.
Subject Matter
No pictorial scene — the piece is otherwise entirely plain/undecorated apart from a single ornate calligraphic flourish in blue ink near one end.
Inscriptions
  • “S.O.E. (tentative reading, per Mike Cornelia)” (in blue ink near one end of the strip's face)
    An interlocking monogram of initials rendered in heavy Spencerian/copperplate-style calligraphic flourish, likely belonging to the intended recipient (sweetheart) of the busk. Read by Mike Cornelia as 'S.O.E.'; the extreme decorative looping made this difficult to resolve independently.
Artist Attribution
unknown
Date / Period
undated (stylistic estimate: if a corset busk, 19th century, consistent with the heyday of sailor-made scrimshaw busks as sweetheart gifts)
Mount / Display Stand
None — displayed flat/unmounted.
Color / Technique
Blue ink calligraphic monogram only; no pictorial engraving elsewhere on the piece
Condition Notes
Surface appears clean, glossy, and intact with no visible cracks or damage. Not physically examined.
Distinguishing Features
Unlike nearly every other piece in the collection, this object bears no pictorial scene at all — only a monogrammed initials flourish. A confirmed corset busk, a classic form of sailor-made sweetheart scrimshaw, distinct in object category from the mounted teeth, plaques, and tools seen elsewhere in the collection.
Legal / Regulatory Status
Material and species not confirmed (ivory vs. bone). If ivory postdates 1972, MMPA restrictions would apply; if antique/pre-1972 (consistent with a 19th-century busk), different rules apply. Flag for legal review before any loan/sale; not a legal determination.
